Build modules in detail · 1 of 3

Kynex by QI SpineLaunch plan · v2 · 28 Nov 2026

Every module takes 3 weeks: week 1 build, week 2 build and connect, week 3 test and polish. Track A (patient side) and Track B (clinic side) run side by side. The Project Head sets the team size and accepts each module at its review.

Release pipelineAutomatic tests and build on every change. Releases need the Project Head's approval.No release can go out without passing tests and the Project Head's sign-off.
Sign-in & accountsPatients sign in with their mobile number and a one-time code. Staff sign in with email and a second step. Roles: patient, physio/doctor, centre admin, management. Lockout and session timeout.Every role sees only what it should. Tested on low-end phones.

Roles and responsibilities

Who does what

Kynex by QI SpineLaunch plan · v2 · 28 Nov 2026

Project Head: product manager & core builder

  • Only point of contact between the project and management
  • Owns the vision, roadmap, scope and priorities. Every change request comes through the Project Head.
  • Decides the team size and how developers are split across the two build tracks
  • Writes the specs and acceptance criteria for every module
  • Builds and owns the camera engine, the AI and the architecture
  • Approves every design, code merge, content item and release
  • Runs module planning and reviews, the Friday updates and the demos
  • Has admin access to every company account (cloud, stores, repository, payments)

Management (sponsor)

  • Approves the plan, the budget and the team allocation
  • Unblocks access to other departments (IT, Legal, centres)
  • Attends the demos and takes the go/no-go decision with the Project Head

UI/UX designer

  • Phase 1: research, user flows, wireframes and the 'Kynex by QI Spine' design system
  • Phase 2: final screens, one module ahead of development, and a usability test after each module
  • Phase 3: visual polish, motion, accessibility and the final usability round
  • Works from the Project Head's specs and to the Project Head's sign-off

Developers (2 build tracks)

  • Build each 3-week module from the Project Head's specs, in the Kynex codebase
  • Track A builds the patient app, care and communication, languages and the mobile apps
  • Track B builds the doctor dashboard, bookings, payments, clinical insights, admin and the CRM
  • Follow the Project Head's code review and release process

Physio team (2-3, 1-2 days a week)

  • Hand over the QI Spine exercise database (100+ exercises)
  • Define the rules and form criteria for each exercise
  • Review the AI instructor videos, or do the physio shoot if it's needed
  • Choose the outcome measures, write the education content, and review clinical text in each language
  • Run the clinical acceptance tests

QA

  • Write the test plan and run the low-end Android device lab
  • Test in week 3 of every module, then run the full regression and the release checks
  • Monitor the dry run

Company IT & Legal

  • Set up the cloud, store, payment, WhatsApp and video accounts in the company's name, with the Project Head as admin
  • Provide CRM access and the security test
  • Legal: DPDP, consent, privacy policy, terms and any medical-device question

Centre staff

  • Take part in the dry run and the training
  • Onboard patients and give first-line support

How the team works

  1. All work enters through the Project Head's backlog. Nobody changes scope, design or priorities without the Project Head's approval.
  2. 3-week build modules on two tracks: the Project Head scopes each module, the team builds and tests it, and the Project Head accepts it at the review.
  3. Done means built, tested by QA, checked by a physio where it's clinical, and approved by the Project Head.
  4. Only the Project Head reports to management: the Friday update, the demos and decisions.
  5. Code, cloud, stores and data stay in company accounts, with the Project Head as admin. Every release goes through the Project Head.

What could slow us down

Risks and decisions

Kynex by QI SpineLaunch plan · v2 · 28 Nov 2026
RiskLevelHow it's handled
Two build tracks need enough developersHighAbout 2 developers per track. The Project Head sets the team size in week 1. With fewer developers, modules run one after another and the go-live date is reviewed at Demo 2.
Very large scope for 18 weeksHigh3-week modules with a test week each, feature freeze on 26 Feb, a follow-up release lane, and a weekly scope review by the Project Head
Shared team members get pulled onto other workHighManagement names each person and their % time in week 1, committed until 30 Apr
QI Spine exercise database arrives lateHighNeeded by 18 Dec. The Project Head escalates to management if it slips.
AI videos not approved for 100+ exercisesMediumA 10-exercise pilot and a decision by 8 Jan. A physio shoot (7-8 days over 5 weeks) is kept as the backup.
Delays opening WhatsApp, payment, video and CRM accessMediumApplications go in during week 2, before the modules that need them. Company IT owns them.
Apple / Google review delaysMediumSubmit by 26 Feb, leaving 3 weeks of buffer. Patients can use the web app link either way.
Translation quality across 12-17 languagesMediumAI translation plus native-speaker review. Centre-region languages are done first.
Camera speed on low-end Android phonesMediumDevice lab from week 2. The Project Head owns the engine.
Centre staff slow to adopt the appMediumDry run, training at every centre, helpdesk and 4 weeks of hypercare
Holiday weeks (Christmas, Sankranti, Holi/Good Friday)LowAlready built into the plan. Holi week is buffer only.

Decisions needed from management in week 1

  1. Approve this plan and the Project Head charter: single point of contact, with final say on scope, priorities, team size and releases
  2. Name the team members and their % time, committed until 30 Apr 2027. About 4 developers allows two build tracks.
  3. Approve the budget (cost list presented in week 1)
  4. Name the contacts for IT/cloud, CRM and Legal
  5. Confirm the list of centres, which sets the regional languages
  6. Decide who staffs the patient helpdesk and whether current patients are imported
  7. Agree the reporting rhythm and whether to build a project tracker app

Measuring the launch

Targets, holidays and assumptions

Kynex by QI SpineLaunch plan · v2 · 28 Nov 2026

Launch success targets (proposed, to confirm in week 1)

Centres live

Every QI Spine centre is live on 31 Mar 2027

Ease of use

90%+ of usability-test users, including those aged 60+, finish key tasks without help

Speed

A patient is onboarded in under 3 minutes; a doctor prescribes a plan in under 2 minutes

Adoption

70% of new rehab patients at live centres are onboarded in the first 4 weeks

Adherence

60%+ of patients exercise at least 3 times a week

Quality

Crash-free sessions at 99.5% or above; app store rating of 4.5 or above

Support

Critical issues are fixed within 24 hours during hypercare

Planning assumptions

  • The team starts on Tue 1 Dec 2026. Week 0 (this week) is for preparation and the pitch to management.
  • Every build module takes 3 weeks: week 1 build, week 2 build and connect, week 3 test and polish
  • Two build tracks run side by side and need about 4 developers. The Project Head decides the team size, and that sets the speed.
  • Team: 1 UI/UX designer, 2-3 physios at 1-2 days a week and 1 QA, all on board by week 2
  • UX works in three phases: structure first (research, flows, wireframes), screens one module ahead, then a long polish phase
  • Exercise videos are AI-generated by preference. A physio shoot of 100+ exercises (7-8 shoot days over 5 weeks) is the backup.
  • The phone apps wrap the existing Kynex web app; we are not writing separate native apps
  • Chat, video consults, payments and WhatsApp use ready-made services rather than being built from scratch
  • Languages: AI translation plus native-speaker review. Centre-region languages are done first.
  • All accounts are owned by the company, with the Project Head as admin
  • The success targets are proposals, to be confirmed with management in week 1
