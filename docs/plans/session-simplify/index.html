A simpler session: less to read, more to see

From the latest feedback on the app. Nothing here is built yet. Once you approve the mockups and the change list, we build it, then demo it next week.

“90% of UI/UX is decluttering. If there's not too much to read, the brain automatically knows what to do.”

Today the patient answers a pain score and five separate yes/no questions, then reads a screen with key points, phone placement, steps, safety, camera and voice toggles and three buttons. The proposal asks for the same information in one sheet and shows the movement instead of describing it.

What stays exactly the same

  • Any safety “yes” stops the session and alerts the clinic.
  • Rep counting, form checks, holds, rest timer and the session report.
  • “Count by tapping” for anyone without a camera.
  • Videos stay on the phone unless the patient opts in.
  • English and Hindi now, with more languages later.

Out of scope for this change

Edge Vitals-style face-scan vitals: interesting, but a separate product idea. It's parked in the ideas list, not in this build.

How it works

  • The phone already records the session and tracks body points. The replay plays on the phone; nothing new is uploaded.
  • When a form rule breaks (shoulder flexion: elbow bent below 140°, or leaning back), the app takes the last 2 seconds and zooms on that joint.
  • The guide is zoomed on the same joint at the same point in the movement.
  • The cue line and the voice come from the same rule: one message, not three.

Keeping it simple

  • Every wrong rep is highlighted before the next rep.
  • The first time a mistake happens in a set, a full slow-motion replay. If the same mistake happens again, a quick 1.5-second zoomed highlight with the cue.
  • Say “carry on” or tap to skip.

To check while building

Zooming needs a sharp picture. On older phones we may show the zoomed body points only. We'll test on a low-end Android first.

Fixed phrases plus close variations. Free to run.

When it listens

  • Fully during pauses: highlights, rest, between exercises.
  • During reps it hears only “stop”, “pause” and “it hurts”, so it doesn't react to its own voice.
  • A mic icon shows whenever it is listening. Audio isn't stored; only the command is.

Phase 2: AI conversation

Open questions answered by an AI coach, held to physio-approved content and safety rules. It has a running cost, so it needs budget approval and physio sign-off first.

Decisions needed from you

  • Approve the screens as drawn, or mark changes.
  • Safety checks as one list: is one “None of these” tap acceptable, or must each question be answered? This needs a physio and legal OK.
  • Terms: agree once at sign-up and show a one-line reminder each session (proposed), or agree every session?
  • Reference video: use the animated figure for the demo until the AI or physio videos are ready?
  • Repeated mistakes: full replay only the first time, then a quick highlight (proposed), or a full replay every time?
  • Voice in the web link: browser speech recognition may send audio to Google. Allow it with a privacy note, or voice only in the Android and iPhone apps?
  • AI voice coach (phase 2): add to the week-1 budget request?
  • Split direction: camera on top (as asked) on phones; side by side on tablets and laptops?

How we'll work

Plan and mockups first, on Claude Sonnet 5.5. Nothing is built until you approve. Then development switches to Claude Haiku 5.5, one change at a time, each shown to you before the next.

Video storage, later

Storing videos needs patient consent (the waiver), a privacy policy update and India-region storage (DPDP). It is heavy on storage, so it's planned as its own step.
