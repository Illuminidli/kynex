Why Kynex

Most rehab happens at home, where nobody can see it.

Physiotherapy works when patients do the right exercises, the right way, often enough. Between clinic visits, that part is invisible to the patient and to the physiotherapist.

Done wrong, or not at all

Patients go home with a printed sheet. They forget steps, cut the movement short, or stop when it gets dull.

Physios can't see between visits

They learn what happened at the next appointment, from memory. A problem that could be fixed in a day goes unnoticed for weeks.

Apps that leave people out

Most digital rehab expects a young, English-speaking user on a new phone. Many patients are older, speak other languages and use budget Android phones.

Kynex in one sentence

The phone's camera becomes the patient's exercise coach. It counts every rep, corrects form out loud in their own language, and gives the clinic a clear picture of how recovery is going.

How it works

1

Assess

The physio sees the patient and records pain, function and goals.

2

3

Patients

One button to start today's session, a coach that speaks their language, and progress in plain words.

Physiotherapists

A list sorted by who needs attention, a replay of every session, and plans that take seconds to change.

Clinic teams

Bookings, payments and reminders in one place, plus a live view of how patients and centres are doing.

The camera coach

A coach that watches every rep, and keeps the video on the phone

Kynex tracks the body's joints from the phone camera, on the phone itself. Each exercise carries its own clinical rules: which joints matter, what a full rep looks like, and which mistakes to watch for.

Counts every rep

Full and partial reps, holds in seconds, sets, rest and each side separately. If it can't see you, it says what to fix: "Step back, I can't see your feet."

Catches form slips

Hips turning, knees falling in, moving too fast. One short, specific tip at a time, spoken and shown, without nagging.

Speaks your language

Counts, cues and corrections in a natural voice. English and Hindi today, with more Indian languages Planned

Private by design

Video never leaves the patient's phone. Only joint angles and counts reach the clinic: enough to replay the movement on a 3D figure.

What the physio sees

A replay of the whole session from the joint data. Every rep sits on a timeline, form problems are marked, and the range reached is shown against the target.

Built so far

Built
  • Live pose tracking on the phone, front or back camera
  • Counting rules, form checks and phone placement for 25 exercises
  • Spoken counts and tips in English and Hindi
  • Session reports, rep replay, and a full movement replay for physios

Next

Planned

The coach counts and gives tips. It does not diagnose, and it does not confirm that technique is clinically correct. The physiotherapist stays in charge of the plan.

How we design

Calm, minimal and usable by anyone

One action per screen

Each screen answers "what do I do now?" with one obvious button. Everything else stays secondary, or out of sight until it's needed.

Designed for 65, pleasant at 25

Large type, strong contrast, big tap targets and plain words. Tested with older and less tech-confident patients throughout.

Automate, don't ask

Reminders, questionnaires, education and follow-ups happen on schedule. Patients and physios do less admin, not more.

Built for India's phones

Smooth on budget Android phones, forgiving of weak internet, and in the patient's own language, voice included.

Trust & safety

Physios stay in charge. Patients stay safe.

  • Red-flag checks before every session. A worrying answer stops the session and alerts the clinic.
  • Physio-owned plans. The app never changes a plan on its own.
  • Video stays on the phone. Only joint data and counts are shared.
  • Consent and privacy built in, with every change to a patient's care recorded.
  • Role-based access: patients, physios, centre teams and management each see only what they need.
  • Honest about limits: the coach counts and gives tips; it does not diagnose.
